The Red Planet: Mars

Unveiling the mysteries of Earth's next-door neighbour

1. Introduction

Mars, the fourth planet from the Sun, is a terrestrial planet with a cold desert climate. Known as the "Red Planet" due to iron oxide on its surface, it shares some similarities with Earth, yet presents a vastly different environment.

Its gravity is about 38% that of Earth:

\[ \frac{g_{Mars}}{g_{Earth}} = \frac{3.71}{9.81} \approx 0.38 \]

2. Basic Facts About Mars

  • Distance from Sun: \( 2.279 \times 10^8 \, \text{km} \)
  • Orbital period: 687 Earth days
  • Diameter: 6,779 km
  • Moons: Phobos and Deimos
  • Average temperature: ~\(-63^\circ\)C
  • Atmosphere: Thin, mostly CO₂
  • Gravity: 3.721 m/s²

3. Surface and Geography

Key features include:

  • Olympus Mons: Tallest volcano in the Solar System (\(21.9 \, \text{km}\))
  • Valles Marineris: A canyon system over 4,000 km long
  • Hellas Planitia: Deepest impact basin

4. Climate and Atmosphere

Mars has a very thin atmosphere composed of:

  • 95.3% Carbon dioxide
  • 2.7% Nitrogen
  • 1.6% Argon

Surface pressure is ~610 Pa (compared to Earth’s 101,325 Pa).

Blackbody temperature estimate:

\[ T = \left( \frac{(1 - A) \cdot S}{4\sigma} \right)^{1/4} \]

Where \( A = 0.25 \), \( S = 590 \, \text{W/m}^2 \), \( \sigma \): Stefan–Boltzmann constant

5. Water on Mars

Signs of water include polar ice caps, hydrated salts, and radar detection of subsurface lakes. Ancient riverbeds and deltas support theories of a once wetter Mars.

6. Life on Mars?

While no life has been confirmed, Curiosity has found organic molecules. Seasonal methane spikes raise the possibility of either biological or geological processes.

7. Mars Missions

  • Viking (1976) — First successful landers
  • Pathfinder (1997) — Sojourner rover
  • Curiosity (2012) — Gale Crater exploration
  • InSight (2018) — Marsquake monitoring
  • Perseverance (2021) — Searching for biosignatures
  • Mangalyaan (2014) — Indian orbiter mission
  • Tianwen-1 (2021) — China’s orbiter, lander & rover

8. Human Mission Possibilities

NASA and SpaceX envision sending humans to Mars in the 2030s–2040s. Major challenges include radiation, food supply, psychological effects, and entry/descent/landing technology.

9. Terraforming Mars

Possible strategies include:

  • Warming polar caps using orbital mirrors
  • Introducing super greenhouse gases
  • Constructing magnetic fields to prevent atmospheric loss

Estimated energy needed: \( 10^{24} \, \text{J} \), far beyond current capabilities.

10. Conclusion

Mars stands as humanity's top candidate for interplanetary colonization. With continued exploration, we may soon witness the first steps on another world — paving the way for a multiplanetary future.
